3/24/2025 ## Storytelling essentials

- Do I know my [novel’s perspective](https://nathanbransford.com/all-about-perspectives-in-novels)? Is it completely consistent? Do I really understand [the difference between third person omniscient, third person limited, and head jumping/hopping](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/08/third-person-omniscient-vs-limited-vs-head-jumping)? Really really?
- Do [I have a plot](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2008/06/does-your-novel-have-plot)?
- Does my protagonist(s) [want something](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2009/03/what-do-your-characters-want)? Are [the stakes](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/10/how-to-raise-the-stakes-in-a-novel) clear to the reader?
- Is [change underway in my setting](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/10/what-makes-great-setting) that impacts the characters?
- Do my characters face [obstacles of increasing intensity](https://nathanbransford.com/2019/09/how-to-create-a-great-villain)?
- Does my protagonist(s) [emerge changed](https://nathanbransford.com/2020/01/what-is-a-character-arc)?

## Structure and organization

- Are my characters [actively going after things they want](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/01/why-protagonists-need-to-be-active) in every single scene? [Even if they’re shy](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/07/shy-characters-dont-have-to-be-passive) or [adrift/depressed/aimless](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/12/how-to-write-a-character-whos-adrift)?
- Does my [main plot arc initiate close enough to the beginning](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/04/everything-you-need-to-know-about-inciting-incidents) that I won’t lose the reader? Should I include a [mini-quest](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/05/give-your-protagonist-a-mini-quest-before-the-main-plot-kicks-off) before the main plot kicks off to keep the protagonist active?
- Are my [chapters well-organized](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/05/how-to-organize-a-chapter)? Do they reach a definitive moment of punctuation or do they fizzle out?
- Does my protagonist(s) [alternate between up and down moments](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/06/how-to-outline-a-novel), with the most intense towards the end?
- Is the [pacing](https://nathanbransford.com/2007/03/setting-the-pace.html) correct for my genre? Is it consistent?
- Are [momentous events given the weight they deserve?](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/03/how-to-show-a-character-reacting-to-a-dramatic-moment) Am I being conscious about [what is dramatized and what happens off the page](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/07/what-happens-on-and-off-the-page-in-your-novel)?
- If I can take out a chapter and the plot will still make sense, is that chapter really necessary? Should some events be folded in with others?
- Does my book come to a [satisfying climax](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/06/how-to-write-a-good-climax-in-a-novel)? Does the ending feel rushed?

## Characters and relationships

- Do I have enough [conflict](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2009/03/on-conflict)?
- Does the reader see the [best and worst characteristics](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/09/dont-be-too-easy-on-your-characters) of my main characters? Are their [strengths balanced by weaknesses](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/06/list-of-character-strengths-and-weaknesses)? Am I being too easy on them?
- Is my protagonist(s) engaging or am I [risking “losing” the reader](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2011/07/what-makes-characters-sympathetic-and) with actions that are beyond the pale?
- Do my characters have specific [hopes and dreams](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/09/how-to-use-hopes-and-dreams-to-make-a-character-come-alive)?
- Do the relationships between my characters [change and become more complicated as the book goes on](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/07/how-to-spice-up-relationships-in-novels)?
- Do conflicts between characters end in the same muddled place or are [conflicts allowed to linger](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/02/dont-release-the-tension)? Are there [ups and downs](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2010/04/john-green-and-dynamic-character)? Do the characters have to work hard to achieve resolutions?
- Do my characters have backstories and histories? Do these impact the plot? [Do I really need them on the page](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/05/how-to-utilize-exposition-and-context-in-a-novel)?
- Do any of my characters [feel flat](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/09/7-reasons-your-characters-feel-flat)? Do they need to be [spiced up](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/01/9-ways-to-spice-up-characters)?
- Are the [adults running away with my children’s novel](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/04/how-to-write-adult-characters-in-childrens-books)?

## Craft and prose

- Am I [over-relying on dialogue](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2015/03/4-ways-to-avoid-screenplayizing-your)? Do the [conversations have a point](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2010/09/seven-keys-to-writing-good-dialogue)? Are my protagonist(s) motivations and thought processes clear from [the narrative voice](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/08/what-is-the-narrative-voice)?
- Am I providing adequate [physical description](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/03/how-to-write-clear-physical-description) to immerse the reader in the setting? Am I describing [new characters and settings when they’re first introduced](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/02/describe-characters-and-settings-when-theyre-first-introduced)? Is it clear [how characters are moving from Point A to Point B](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/02/show-characters-getting-from-point-a-to-point-b)?
- Am I [orienting the reader](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/05/dont-start-a-scene-without-these-four-essential-elements) at the start of new scenes?
- Am I giving the reader [sufficient context to understand what’s happening](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/05/how-to-utilize-exposition-and-context-in-a-novel)? Am I bogging things down with [aimless info-dumps](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/07/dont-build-scenes-around-the-information-you-think-you-need-to-impart)?
- Are my [mysteries chosen judiciously](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/02/how-to-craft-mystery-in-novel) or am I [being so vague](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/09/are-you-creating-a-mystery-or-just-being-vague) I’m going to confuse the reader?
- Are my gestures [specific and individualized](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/03/avoid-these-generic-reactions)? Am I including [aimless stage direction](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2022/06/avoid-aimless-stage-direction)?
- Do I know my [writing tics](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2010/08/do-you-suffer-from-one-of-these-writing-2)? Is my [voice](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2010/05/how-to-craft-great-voice) consistent?
- Is my prose belabored with [excessive words or explanations that are already apparent from context](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/08/what-to-cut-when-your-book-is-too-long)? Am I [over-explaining “default” objects](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/08/dont-over-explain-default-objects-and-gestures)?
- Am I using active verbs and [clearing out the clutter in my prose](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/02/clear-out-the-clutter-around-your-verbs)?

## Brass tacks

- Is my [novel formatted correctly](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/10/how-to-format-a-manuscript)?
- If I’m pursuing traditional publishing, does [the first installment in a series](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/03/everything-writers-need-to-know-about-book-series) come to a completely satisfying conclusion that does not depend on a sequel?
- Do I [know my genre](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2018/04/why-its-important-to-know-your-genre)?
- Is my [word count](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2018/04/all-about-novel-word-counts) appropriate for my genre?
- Is my [novel’s title](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2020/03/how-to-choose-a-book-title) evocative and genre-appropriate?

All of this is easier said than done! It’s so difficult to see what is and isn’t on the page, which is why [seeking feedback is important](https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2019/05/how-to-find-and-work-with-a-book-editor). But the farther you can get self-editing on your own, the more you’ll get out of the editing process.


## Why You Should Care About Plot Holes

Finding and fixing plot holes matters because they affect your readers’ experience and enjoyment of your story — to the point where they may stop reading (or grumble about them on Goodreads.)

When your readers stumble across a plot hole, it feels like they’ve hit a speed bump. It feels jarring, and they’re thrown out of the story world.

This is bad enough, but worse, they might start to think you don’t know what you’re doing. And if they don’t trust you as an author, they’re going to find it hard to enjoy your novels.

So in general, it is good practice to avoid them as much as possible.
